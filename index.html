CPE102L-4 — Programming Logic & Design

Number Systems & Notations Calculator

The problemPostfix/prefix evaluation, infix reconstruction, and base conversion are usually taught — and tested — as four separate exercises, so it's easy to get each one individually right and the pipeline collectively wrong.
The approachBuilt the logic in Flowgorithm first, then ported it to Python: a stack evaluates the expression, the evaluation trace reconstructs the fully-parenthesized infix form, and the result converts to binary, hex, and octal in one pass — with input validation that retries on a malformed expression instead of crashing.
ValidationEvery test case, including negative results, was cross-checked live against three independent reference tools (a notation visualizer, a stack visualizer, a hex converter) until all three agreed.

GED106 — Purposive Communication

SentryBand Technical Report

A wrist-worn safety concept for the engineers and oilers standing watch in a ship's engine room, developed as an APA 7 technical report and a NABC pitch (Need · Approach · Benefit · Competition).

The needRoughly 350 seafarers and shore workers have died from enclosed-space asphyxiation since 1996, 70 of those in just the last three years — and about 95% of fatal ferry incidents happen on smaller domestic-route vessels that don't carry the fixed monitoring larger ships already have.
The approachSentryBand fuses heat-strain sensing (skin temperature and heart rate) with gas sensing (carbon monoxide, hydrogen sulfide, and combustible vapors) on an ESP32 microcontroller, checks every reading against the NIOSH heat framework and standard gas-exposure thresholds, and escalates from a vibration alert to a full vibration/LED/buzzer alarm with a Bluetooth Low Energy notification to the control-room dashboard — sealed in an IP65-rated housing, running a full watch on a rechargeable Li-ion battery.
BenchmarkedPositioned in the NABC pitch against the three categories already in use — fixed monitoring systems, portable gas detectors, and wearable health trackers — none of which combine continuous dual-hazard sensing with engine-room-rated (IP65) wear.
FormatAPA 7 report + NABC pitch deck & script
SensingHeat-strain (skin temp + heart rate) & gas (CO, H₂S, combustible vapors) on an ESP32

CM011 — Special Topics in General Chemistry

Semiconductor Fabrication Chemistry

A Module 3 technical presentation tracing chip manufacturing back to the chemistry underneath it — three operations repeated 30–100+ times to build a single wafer.

The problemChip manufacturing looks like a mechanical problem from the outside, but patterning, doping, and etching are each a chemistry problem underneath — and that chemistry is what actually limits performance, cost, and availability.
The approachTraced the cycle chemically: positive/negative photoresist chemistry in photolithography, diffusion vs. ion-implantation doping, and wet vs. dry (plasma) etching — then worked the Rayleigh criterion to show why EUV's 13.5 nm wavelength prints ~3.5× smaller features than 193 nm DUV, and the implant-dose equation to show why independently-tunable dose and depth displaced diffusion for critical junctions.
Why it mattersCloses on the environmental cost — about 185M tons CO₂-equivalent a year, roughly half of per-wafer chemical use from wet processing — and why that chemistry sets a hard ceiling on chip cost, performance, and where fabs can be built.

Worked calculationsRayleigh criterion (CD = k₁·λ/NA) & implant dose (Q = It/qA)

About

Who I am and how I got here.

I'm Marpel — a BS Marine Engineering graduate now serving as 4th Engineer aboard an international vessel, and studying BS Computer Engineering fully online at Mapúa University in whatever hours the watch schedule leaves free.

Marine engineering taught me to think in safety margins and failure modes. Computer engineering is where I'm learning to build the embedded tools that watch over the people still standing watch — starting with SentryBand, a wearable heat-strain and toxic-gas monitor for the engine room.

Most of the coursework happens in whatever connectivity the ship allows between watches — Flowgorithm and Python for the logic-and-design work, GitHub and Claude Code to keep the workflow moving from one port to the next.

The sea time backs up the marine-engineering side of that: bulk carriers and container ships, worldwide trade, working up from Engine Cadet to Oiler across contracts that have run through the Suez Canal, Singapore, Istanbul, and a string of Chinese ports since 2022. It's MARINA/STCW-certified — Basic Training, Advanced Fire Fighting, Medical First Aid, Survival Craft & Rescue Boat, Ship Security Awareness, and Ratings Forming Part of an Engineering Watch.

Personal Journey

From engine room to CpE classroom

Studying on a 4-on/8-off watch schedule, with whatever bandwidth a ship at sea can spare.

Studying Computer Engineering on a four-on, eight-off watch schedule means time isn't mine to plan — it's fragmented by alarms, maintenance, and exhaustion. The real challenge wasn't the coursework; it was the isolation. Over erratic satellite bandwidth, there's no forum thread to check mid-ocean when a build breaks. I learned to debug by hand: tracing logic on paper, reading offline manuals, and verifying algorithms before ever touching a terminal.

That habit reshaped how I think. Long night watches taught me to shift cleanly from deck-side alertness to deep focus at a desk, even on broken sleep. Out there, reliability isn't abstract — it's the line between systems that hold and ones that fail. That mindset now drives how I approach every line of code.
